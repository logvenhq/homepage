The concept

Logven is an operational platform for defining, executing, observing, and formalizing enterprise processes. It is designed for complex technical operations that cannot be fully specified in advance.

The problem

Enterprise software initiatives often require complete process specifications before implementation begins. In practice, dependencies, exceptions, approval paths, and edge cases frequently become visible only during execution.

Large organizations must translate these evolving processes into reliable systems that can support tens of thousands of employees. They must also meet requirements for governance, data residency, security, auditability, and on-premises deployment.

The solution

Logven uses LLM-powered agents to create an executable first model of a process before every workflow detail is known. Each execution produces operational evidence that shows how the work is performed in practice.

An initial agent represents a testable hypothesis about how a task should be performed. Running that hypothesis against real work exposes missing inputs, failure modes, decision points, and control requirements.

Over time, execution data provides the basis for converting variable workflows into standardized processes and, ultimately, purpose-built operational software.

User-modeled agents are therefore an intermediate representation, not the target architecture. As process knowledge matures, Logven progressively compiles stable behavior into private, deterministic software. Smaller language models, ideally below 40 billion parameters, remain only where probabilistic reasoning provides measurable value.

We view agents as a transitional mechanism for process discovery rather than a permanent abstraction for every system component. They make an incomplete workflow executable and observable; once behavior becomes stable and well understood, conventional software components replace the corresponding agentic steps.

An example

We explored this approach with a geothermal team in the public sector.

The team manages data from 36 geothermal plants. Its workflow includes extracting values from PDFs, updating spreadsheets and databases, consolidating and analyzing data, producing reports, processing responses, and resolving anomalies.

As the system observes more executions, Logven can consolidate equivalent agents, establish canonical data models, introduce approval controls at validated decision points, and replace variable agent handoffs with deterministic code and system integrations.

Language models remain only in workflow segments where material ambiguity persists.

The resulting architecture is no longer primarily agent-based. It becomes a dedicated plant-operations system that converges from observed work rather than relying on a complete upfront specification.

This convergence reduces model dependence, improves predictability, and lowers inference and operational costs.

Additional outcomes

The operational record also provides a clearer basis for workforce planning. When an employee leaves or changes role, the organization can see the responsibilities, dependencies, and decision points that person occupied within the team. This supports a more accurate handover and a more precise definition of the role to be filled.

Across the organization, the same record provides a clearer picture of how the company operates in practice: where responsibilities sit, how teams and systems depend on one another, and where work is duplicated or constrained. This gives management a firmer basis for organizational and operational decisions.

Team

We develop Logven in close collaboration with the technical teams that operate it. Direct discovery, production workflows, and early deployments in hard-tech environments inform the product architecture.

We maintain a focused team and work directly with the operations the platform is designed to improve.
